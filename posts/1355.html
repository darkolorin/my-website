OpenAI put out a blog post (https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier/) there, a good one. Like, exactly on point so you don’t go thinking “haha who needs your inference, just throw an agent at it and you’re done.”
Good quote, we’re seeing something similar:
Agents now complete nearly half of two-to-four-hour research tasks on their own. But humans are still central to AI development.
From March to October, estimated success rose from 23% to 46% for tasks estimated to require two to four hours of human work, and rose from 13% to 35% for tasks estimated to require four to eight hours. But the kind of work matters. For example, in a task-level breakdown, the autonomous agent success rate was 79% for monitoring training runs, 35% for launching training runs, and 25% for writing code for inference and serving stacks. Overall, uplift is jagged, and humans remain a core part of the AI research loop.
Roughly speaking, in our tasks the proportions are the same. Anyone over there who believes in full automation of programming probably needs to wait a bit. But at the same time for intranet stuff and all sorts of landing pages I don’t see any point in sweating it anymore.
We’re also trying to use various swarm agent things. But for now it’s more for research.
